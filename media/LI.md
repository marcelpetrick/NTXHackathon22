// October was always some kind of Hacktoberfest. Four years ago I joined a hackathon with Shuhong and Tom, where we tried to get any of the provided brain-computer interfaces to work. It was nice that such an event was organized by the TUM. I guess there is also a really old post about this here on LinkedIn. Anyway: at that time we spent most of the time configuring and getting familiar with the hardware and the whole workflow, instead of building a final prototype. So in the afternoon <-- check: around noon (the blink test was recorded on Sunday at 12:45) of Sunday we decided to capture some "control EEG". I never got around to processing them and fixing them against <-- check: testing them against the idea of controlling Pacman with them. But since I came across this GitHub repo while cleaning up, I thought: why not now? So, here it is. One stream contained ten blink signals (with the eyes), which were also recorded by the Ganglion BCI <-- check: OpenBCI Ganglion board we used at that time. They are recognizable by a filter TODO (write more about which type of filtering this is) <-- check: a 0.5–10 Hz band-pass filter, averaged over the four channels, then a robust z-score threshold (median/MAD) with peak picking. Then those impulses are used to control Pacman. As you can see, the result is not optimal and not suited to really "play" that one-dimensional game. But it shows the whole end-to-end workflow. // Now updated on GitHub as a repo, with the tooling, the recorded test data and also the end-to-end connection.
